**Day 1. Wonders of Giza and Cairo**

Our family of 4 arrived early in Cairo on 13th February 2025 and proceeded to meet our guide Asma and explored the **Great Pyramids of Giza**, the **Sphinx**, and the step pyramids of **Saqqara** all situated in close proximity.  The early start beat the crowds and the heat.This experience of standing before these touring structures was intense as Ireflected on my school days when I first heard of the country Egypt and thegreat Pyramids.  The walk around withoutthe intense heat was soul satisfying.

Our young lady, **Lekrisha** was very unhappy as she had no time to freshen up at the hotel as previously indicated on the itinerary by the local travel agent, “**Magic Carpet**”.  This was however not communicated to the driver and the hectic morning traffic also complicated matters. There were average public facilities near the ticket kiosks to quickly freshen up.

We explored the treasures of the **Grand Egyptian Museum** which is dedicated to a single civilisation.   A huge granite statue of the **Pharaoh Ramesses II** is at the entrance of the museum.  The museum also has the various burial objects of King **Tutankhamun**, including his solid-gold funerary mask, golden chariots and golden throne.   This was the beginning of many stories that were to be told about Tutankhamun during our stay in Egypt.  

**On day 2** we took a 1.5-hour flight to **Aswan**, known as the city of gold.  Our licensed English-speaking Egyptologist and guide **Osama** met us with the driver and we set out to explore Aswan.  We visited the beautiful **Philae Temple** (dedicated to the goddess Isis) situated onits own island, and we saw the **Aswan High Dam**.

We also boarded smaller boats and visited a traditional colourful **Nubia village** located on the west bank of the Nile River. Here crocodiles are kept as pets and the practice stems from ancientPharaonic worship of Sobek, the ancient Egyptian crocodile god of the Nile.

 Villagers capture baby crocodiles and raise them in indoor cement tanks, pools, or enclosures.  The lady of the house also put **mehndi** on the palms of the females for a small tip, while the brave tourists carried the crocodiles.  We then boarded our 5-star Nile cruise vessel **HAPPY 5** for 5 course dinner and spent the night aboard.  

**Day 3**: Sailing to **Kom Ombo:**  We stopped to tour the uniquely designed Temple of Kom Ombo, dedicated to the crocodile god Sobek and falcon god Horus. 

**Day 4**:  We had an early startand visited the **Edfu Temple** on the west bank of the river Nile. We took a 20-minute traditional horse-drawn carriage from the riverbanks.  Long queues greeted us before we could even get onto one of the carriages.  

This temple is magnificent and very famous in Egypt as it took about 180 years to complete. The temple is dedicated to Horus (god of sky, war, and kingship), along with Hathor and Harsomtus. Its walls feature detailed inscriptions of the "Sacred Drama", being the mythical battle where Horus defeated his uncle Se as well as ancient creation myths and festival records.

We spent the afternoon relaxing on the sun deck as the ship sailed towards Luxor, passing through the famous **Esna Lock**. Watching the ship navigate this historic lock system from the deck is a great cruise experience and my first.  A late evening walk through the city and the markets taking in the aromas of spices and hookahs was marvellous.  Be cautious of the many touts who are attached to specific stalls and they encourage you to purchase their various items on sale.  I was also impressed with the safety I experienced as the local tourist police who mingle with the crowd but make themselves visible when touts become a nuance.    

The entertainment and cuisine on the Happy Five cruise ship were fantastic.   Our package included a gala night where all the drinks from cocktails to whisky’s were provided.   The staff were also very accommodating when our only vegetarian in our crew felt like eating something special.  

**Day 5**: We experienced the grandeur of **Luxor** after disembarkingto explore the East and west Bank of the Nile to experience the painted tombsof the Valley of the Kings, the grand Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut and the Colossi of Memnon.   We were impressed with the tomb of King Tutankhamun and the beautiful, preserved colours it was preserved in.   We heard of this king from our day we arrived in Egypt.  

We completed our journey on the East Bank exploring the massive **Karnak Temple** Complex which is the largest religious site ever constructed and the beautifully illuminated Luxor Temple.   We were sad to bid farewell to our highly efficient guide Osama.  He was truly inspirational with all round knowledge of Egypt and the world at large.

**Day 6**. We flew back to Cairo and spent some quality time at the Cairo Citadel (Citadel of Saladin) which is a historic medieval Islamic-era fortification in Cairo, Egypt.  It served as the seat of government and the residence of Egypt's rulers for nearly 700 years.

The Hanging Church and Abu Serga Church are two of the oldest and most famous Coptic Christian landmarks, located side-by-side in the historic Coptic Cairo (Old Cairo) district.

A visit to Egypt cannot be completed with testing their authentic long-lasting perfumes.   We visited the **Golden Eagle**, which is known for authentic concentrated perfume oils, custom-blended designer-inspired essences, and pure oudh and musk.  After the taste and aromas, we were tempted to buy all on offer, but the budget and luggage space was limited.   The entertainment and refreshments at the **Alabaster factory** was worth the visit to  explore ancient carved relics.   

We spent the evening sampling local chips, nuts and (I enjoyed the cinnamon tea and milk) We also shopped for clothing and souvenirs at the **Khan EL Khalili Bazaar** which is large and any tourist will enjoy navigating the tiny path ways.
