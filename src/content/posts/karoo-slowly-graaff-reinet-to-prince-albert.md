A family adventure from the 7th to the 10th of August 2026 to one of largest waterfalls, a jeep ride and river float alongside hundreds of elephants culminating in Impalila island in the Namibian desert.This journey took us through the border posts of Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana,and Namibia.

The Double-Sided Wonder of Victoria Falls

The journey began from OR Tambo aboard Fastjet airways.  On landing in Zimbabwe we were  transferred to the luxurious A’zambezi RiverLodge situated in the National Park and on the banks of the Zambezi river.    While seated at the bars and dining area, guests enjoy majestic views of theriver.    After settling in we embarked on a five star dinner experience on the majestic Zambezi river aboard the….  Seeing the sun set in Zambia while enjoying great cuisine for over 3 hours was the topper. 

ON DAY 2, we were transported to Mosi-oa-Tunyaor the—The Smoke That Thunders.  Although a short drive we saw baboons and elephants en route.  To appreciate Victoria Falls, you have to see it from both borders, as each country offers an entirely different view and relationship with the falls.

While going though the Victoria Falls National Park on the Zimbabwean side our local guide pointed out the flora in the well maintained rain forest paths.   While walking along the cliff edge, you face the sprays of water directly and at viewpoints like Danger Point, the large volume of the Zambezi River pouring into the gorge below is a magnificent view and feel. 

Crossing into the Zambia brought us closer to the water flow.The trail here takes you across the Knife Edge Bridge, a narrow footbridge where you are surrounded in down pour. Looking down into the boiling pot below reveals the terrifying speed of the river. 

Just a 10 minute drive from the Zambian side of the falls is a historic town called Livingstone.  It was named after the explorer David Livingstone and the town serves as a peaceful base to unwind from the adrenaline of the falls and enjoy the local brew.  There are statues of Livingstone in the park as well.  The dinner at the lodge ranged from ostrich and crocodile steaks to fish and chicken.  There was always a wide range of desserts to choose from.  The daily entertainment was top class and it was great fun to participate in the dancing and beating of the drums.

Chobe National Park

A quick crossing through the Kazungula border took us into Botswana and straight into Chobe National Park on Day 3. Chobe is known for its wildlife density, specifically its massive population of elephants. 

A morning game drive through the park’s rugged terrain brought us in close proximity to a leopard resting in tree with the remains of its catch, herds of buffalo, few lions who were feasting on its prey and a young cheetah guarding its catch.   This was my time to see a cat at close range.  After midday drinks and snacks in the park we reached our 5 star venue for delicious lunch after 1pm.  

The Namibia Desert

A day trip to Impalila Island in Namibia started with a 20-minute boat ride across the Chobe River from Kasane in Botswana.

After clearing customs, we met our guide David and walked about 20 minutes and did the village walk.  We met the local Subiya community and explored their traditional village.   We learned about their living, fishing traditions and watched traditional dancing by the women.

We also visited the famous 2,000-plus-year-old baobab tree and was able to enter and explore the interior. Due to customs closing earlier during week ends we left this beautiful land and returned to Zimbabwe.

Shopping in Victoria Falls town

There are free shuttle rides into the town and I felt I was in the USA as goods are advertised in dollars and trade is in the US dollars.   We were fortunate to find our regular South African brands like Pick n Pay and we had lunch at Nandos in the Sawanga mall.  

The town has numerous souvenir shops and eateries while tourist operators offer superb discounts towards the latter part of the day.
