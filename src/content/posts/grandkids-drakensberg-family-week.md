**Day 1.**   After disembarking at the Cape Town International Airport, we collected the hired vehicle and visited my wife’s nephew, niece and kid Sonia in Milnerton. After brunch we proceeded to the picturesque **Langebaan just** over an hour from Cape Townwhich was going to be our base for the next 4 nights.  From Langebaan we drifted to the following towns.

**Yzerfontein**

This tranquil fishing village is famous for its uninterrupted beaches, huge homes and a delightfully authentic laid-back vibe like most towns in the West Coast.  Just outside town is the beautifully curated space where you can take a guided tour with San guides to learn about the history, tracking skills, and medicinal plant knowledge of Southern Africa's first people.

**Langebaan & the lagoon**

Langebaan a vibrant coastal town centred around a massive saltwater lagoon. Because the lagoon is protected from the open ocean's wild surf, the water is calm, surprisingly warm, and a paradise for water sports.  Langebaan is the kitesurfing capital of the region and just sitting and watching this spectacle is relaxing.  The town itself is laid back with two malls and many attractions at Club Mykonos which is modeled after the famous Greek island.  This Mykonos themed resort is recognized across South Africa for its distinct whitewashed walls, bright blue shutters, and private marina right on the shores of the Langebaan Lagoon.   

**Paternoster: Whitewashed colourful Charm** 

Paternoster is an old fishing village on the West Coast with a very romantic vibe. It is recognizable by its rows of traditional, lime-washed stone cottages with a blue ocean and traditional wooden fishing boats resting on the sand.  We also visited the nearby Cape Columbine Lighthouse although under construction.. 

**Velddrift: The famous Bokkoms**

 Velddrift is situated at the mouth of the Berg River.  Known as Bokkomlaan, it is a dirt road that runs along the river jetty and is the birthplace of bokkoms—the local delicacy of salted, dried mullet fish or fish biltong as South Africans call it.  The tiny fish is observed being dried in many shacks,.    Even if dried fish isn't to your liking the area is picturesque with lots of dining options or book a relaxing sunset river cruise.

**Darling**: Against the backdrop of the Swartland, lies this darling town.  It is famed for its artistic soul,  and slow-paced West Coast charm, r you are hunting for vintage treasures, craving craft beer, or looking to immerse yourself in political satire and spring wildflowers, this quirky little village packs a major punch.

**\*** Our first stop was the **Darling Museum** which provided insights into 18th and 19th-century village life. Originally founded to celebrate the region's rich dairy heritage, the museum features incredible, authentic exhibits on the old Darling creamery, complete with vintage butter churns, wooden machinery, and re-created Victorian rooms.   Also grab a map at the museum and try out the Darling Mystery Trail, a fun riddle-based walking tour that guides you past the town’s most historic Cape Victorian architecture.   

\* Darling is also famous for South Africa’s most famous "cultural activist," Tannie Evita Bezuidenhout. Created by l satirist Pieter-Dirk Uys, the iconic **Evita se Perron** is housed in the town’s old railway station. It’s a vibrant mix of a theatre, restaurant, garden display, and political exhibition hall. 

**\* Sweets and Darling Craft Brews**

Darling is famous for food and drink:  **Darling Sweet** is noted for reasonably priced handmade toffees. Tourists can observe how the mouth-watering sweets are infused with local flavors like rooibos and Tannie Evita’s choices.   

**Darling Brew**: Regarded a pioneers of South Africa’s craft beer, their taste room is a haven for beer lovers and they also serve delicious meals.  Its a pity we had visited out of the season for the beautiful wild flowers that grow between September to October.  

**St Helena Bay:**  A stop at the Vasco Da Gama Statue is imperative as history underscores that  Vasco Da Gama first set foot here.  The area is not well maintained while the harbour area gives off a strong stench of fish.  

**Cape Met Racing**: After the laid back West Coast we moved back to Kenilworth for the 164th renewal of the World Sports Betting Cape Town Met that took place on Saturday, 31 January 2026, at the Hollywoodbets Kenilworth Racecourse.

I was mesmerized by the theme of  "Symphony of Style,"  and the 12-race card featuring elite Grade 1 contests, live music, movement performers, and high-fashion red carpet spectacles.  My meager winnings covered my drinks and meals for the day.

•	Rent a Car: There is no regular public transport between these small towns. A self-drive road trip is by far the best way to get around.

•	Pack for All Weather: The Atlantic ocean breeze can be incredibly biting, even in the middle of summer. Always pack a windbreaker or jersey with your swimsuit.
