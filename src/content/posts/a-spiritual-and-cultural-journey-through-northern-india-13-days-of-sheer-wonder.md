 **Days 1–3: Arrival and the Sacred Waters of Haridwar& Rishikesh**

Our adventure began upon landing in Delhi on the morning of 27th September 2025.  Our South African agent had assured us of a room to freshen up but availability was a challenge so we freshened up and explored the surroundings at the hotel **Radisson** in IP Extension.  

 

The annual **Ramlila** and Durga Puja festivities was taking place on the local grounds adjacent to the hotel.  When the organisers realised we were tourists, we were treated to front row seats where we observed the various cultural performances.  It was a wonderful way to start our spiritual journey as we also enjoyed the local treats at the various stalls and fun filled atmosphere at the fair. 

The next morning, we were punctually collected by our driver **SriRam** for road trip to **Haridwar**, the "Gateway to God". Watching the evening **Ganga Aarti** at Har ki Pauriwas was an unforgettable introduction to India’s living traditions. We also offered a diya for the well-being of a relative who was experiencing health issues back home.

On Day 3, we took a morning excursion to **Rishikesh**, crossing the iconic **Lakshman Jhula,** suspension bridge.  I visited Rishikesh for the second time in over 10 years and the place had changed radically.  We then set on our seven-hour drive to Mathura.

 **Days 4–5: Krishna’s Heartland, the Taj Mahal, and Royal Lucknow**

Day 4 got us immersed in the mythology of **Mathura** and **Gokul**, the birthplace and childhood home of Lord Krishna, visiting the Dwarkadesh Temple and Vishram Ghat. 

I was also intrigued by the modern **Chaar Dham** which was not developed during my last visit.  This development brings together 4 major sacred abodes namely **Badrinath** (Lord Vishnu), **Dwarka** (Lord Krishna), **Puri** (LordJagannath) and **Rameswaram** (Lord Shiva). There is accommodation on site for devotees but it’s veryexpensive.   We also stayed at the classyNidhivan Sarovar Portico. 

We arrived late at the hotel because I had to pay a tip to the traffic police for our vehicle to go to the hotel.  The hotel is in the middle of the business area so bigger vehicles are restricted entry during peak hours which I thought was ridiculous.   We were later misled by the receptionist who insisted that we have dinner although we were aware it was not included in our package.  We had our dinner en route but sat for a while in the dining area with chai, snacks and live music.  Upon leaving the restaurant we were charged for a full buffet and after protesting we left without incident although the receptionist denied requesting us to have dinner.   

 The next day we left for  **Agra**, and was standing in awe before the white marble majesty of the **Taj Mahal** on the banks of the **Yamuna River**. On October 1, we travelled to **Lucknow**, spending the day exploring its stunning blend of Indo-Islamic and colonial architecture—including the imposing **Bara Imambara** and **Chhota Imambara**—followed by evening shopping in the old city.  Our driver from Delhi was experiencing difficulty in locating places and our intervention with GPS technology assisted somewhat.  We really enjoyed the glamour of the 5-star holiday stay at **Hotel Ramada** by Wyndam in **Lucknow**. 

**Days 6–8: Ancient Temples and Ghats from Ayodhya to Varanasi**

On October 2, we arrived in **Ayodhya** for a spiritually moving visit to the **Ram** **Mandir** which is still under construction.  We enjoyed short visits to **Kanak** **Bhawan**, and the **Saryu River**. 

Ayodhya has a charming way of gently immersing you in the atmosphere and excitement that street vendors bring.   After leaving the **Ram Janmabhoomi** complex,  one lady from our tour party went missing from the crowd.  We spent the best of thirty minutes of engaging with the locals and as we approached th local police she was located much to our delight. That evening we had much to talk and laugh about over strictly vegetarian dinner at the rooftop terrace dining area of the **Ira Orchid hotel**.  It was a pleasure to taste both north and south cuisine.  

In **Prayagraj** we visited the revered **Triveni Sangam**. We went by boats to the confluence of the rivers Ganga, Yamuna and Saraswati.  The water flow and lousy weather avoided us taking a proper dip at this holy spot.

On Day 7, we reached **Varanasi** or **Kashi** as it was known while I was growing up. This brought me great anticipation of what to experience. Our time here spanned two major faiths: we visited Sarnath, where Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon, and paid respects at the sacred Kashi Vishwanath Temple which was magnificent.  The queues we learnt is experienced all year long while many touts offered us quicker access for a fee. 

I enjoyed the views from the boats and stone steps while we witnessed the pyres but we were disappointed that the weather and time did not allow us to experience fully the mesmerizing evening Ganga Aarti that is so highly spoken off.

Our driver was lost looking for our accommodation and dented the vehicle in his anxiety.  We stayed in the heart of the city at the luxurious **Pristine hotel**.  We enjoyed walking and shopping in the well-lit streets of Kashi.  

**Days 9–10: Enlightenment in Bodh Gaya**

On October 5, we drove to Gaya and settled in for a relaxing evening. The following day was dedicated to exploring Bodh Gaya, walking beneath the sacred Bodhi Tree where Buddha attained enlightenment, admiring the Great Buddha Statue, and visiting the Mahabodhi Temple alongside intricate Thai, Japanese, and Bhutanese monasteries.   Nadira and family performed pooja for her late husband Sudesh and it was emotional times for us all.   The late-night shopping and delicious vegetarian meals took away some of the emotional stresses.

 **Days 11–13: Old and New Delhi Finale**


A short flight brought us back to Delhi for the final leg of our trip.  We experienced the vibrant contrasts of the capital from the bustling lanes of Chandni Chowk, Jama Masjid, and Raj Ghat in Old Delhi.  My second visit to the **Raj Ghat** remained nostalgic  as it brough back stories of Gandhi Ji while growing up.   We had quick visits to the towering Qutub Minar, India Gate, and grand government buildings of New Delhi including the residence of the Prime Minister.


We also visited the magnificent **Akshardham.  T**his building kept me in awe as its built without stekll or concrete but intact from 2005.We also visited the **Gurudwara** also known as the **Golden** **temple** where we partook of the daily free kitchen (known as langar).  We experienced sitting and eating on the floor while no photographs were allowed here.  For me it was soothing because of its tranquility, soft live music and simple beauty of the place.  In the evening we walked for about half an hour to the **East Delhi Mall** for final shopping and fast foods.

 On 9th October 2025, we boarded ourflight home, carrying memories of a lifetime.
